# Bun Rust rewrite: update through 30 October 2026

Snapshot: **2026-10-30 09:20:14 UTC (14:20 Almaty)**. Today is incomplete. Part 1 remains an August 10 historical snapshot; its statements about an ongoing release drought are historical, not the current status.

## What changed

**Stable shipping resumed.** Bun 1.4.0 shipped on August 20, 98 calendar days after the rewrite merged. Four patches followed: September 4 (1.4.1), September 5 (1.4.2), October 10 (1.4.3), and October 22 (**1.4.4**, latest observed stable). The original release-drought conclusion no longer describes the present. Sources: [Bun 1.4 announcement](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.4), [release history](https://github.com/oven-sh/bun/releases), [1.4.4 notes](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.4.4).

**The tracked post-merge intake grew to 2,568 issues**, 2,389 of them potential defect reports under the original exclusion-label definition. Of these issues, 1,022 remain open. Since 1.4.0 shipped, the counts are 1,493 issues, 1,385 potential defect reports and 714 open issues. These are counts of reports, not unique confirmed defects. Source: [committed issue snapshot](data/issues.jsonl), collected from [GitHub issue search](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=repo%3Aoven-sh%2Fbun+type%3Aissue+created%3A2026-05-14..2026-10-30).

These values are independently checked against the [slim records](data/issues.jsonl) by `python3 part2/verify.py`; the chart consumes the same [derived payload](data/series.js). A mature report is one opened at least 30 days before the pull. “Fixed” is GitHub's current `closed` / `completed` state, not verification that a fix shipped. Reopened issues count as unresolved; prior closure events cannot be recovered from this snapshot.

**The lower post-stable closure share persists in the age-restricted measure, but the gap is smaller.** The 24.1% post-stable 30-day share is 2.4 percentage points below the frozen baseline and 5.5 below the intervening pre-stable period. This describes issue handling for different cohorts; it does not isolate language effects. The 1.3-day median among completed post-stable reports excludes unresolved reports and must not be interpreted as the typical wait for everyone.

**Keyword counts remain proxies.** Post-stable issues mention crash (133), hang/freeze/deadlock (129), panic (76), segfault/access violation (48), and memory keywords (127). The crash/hang union is **252 distinct issues (16.9%)**, not 262: ten reports match both. Panics can be safe error handling; neither keyword class establishes a memory-safety defect without root-cause evidence. Source: [issue records and flags](data/issues.jsonl).

**Triage is uneven over time.** Unlabelled issues constitute 57.1% of August intake, 15.5% of September and 48.4% of October to date. Thus the old claim of continuously collapsing triage should not be extrapolated. Labels are observed now and can change retrospectively. Bun's [1.4.4 announcement](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.4.4) also reports bulk package-manager and SQL issue closures; these release-note claims are context, not independent validation of our cohort totals.

## Interpretation and limits

The shipping interruption ended, and overall post-merge 30-day closure share (27.2%) is close to the baseline (26.5%). The post-stable subset is lower at 24.1% and has a substantial unresolved intake. The evidence supports continued monitoring of follow-up and release delivery; it cannot establish that Rust improved or worsened underlying defect density.

After August 20, a plain `1.4.x` string no longer identifies canary. The chart's highlighted version subset means a version mention, potentially a dependency version, rather than verified channel membership. All cohort rows above include all builds in their time window.

Report volume combines exposure, defects and reporting behaviour. Without measured exposure/reporting propensities, a report-volume ratio is neither a defect-density estimate nor an unconditional upper bound. Closure shares and latency also depend on issue mix and follow-up. The frozen baseline preserves August states while post-merge states are refreshed; the age-restricted column reduces unequal follow-up but cannot reconstruct reopen histories or eliminate selection bias.

The reconstructed backlog concerns only issues opened after the exact merge timestamp. It is not the repository-wide backlog, and intermediate reopen/reclose events are unavailable. October and today's calendar day are incomplete. This is a multi-request pull, not an atomic database snapshot.

## Collection and reproduction

Six monthly Search API windows returned 2,578 unique records, with `incomplete_results=false` and every page count reconciled to its window total. Ten issues on May 14 preceded the exact merge time (08:09:34 UTC) and were excluded, leaving 2,568. Query ranges and timestamp are saved in [snapshot metadata](data/snapshot.json). Releases were refreshed through the connected GitHub plugin. [Primary-source verification](SOURCES-2026-10-30.md) records the event timeline and limitations.

Run `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile`, then `pnpm run rebuild` and `python3 part2/verify.py`. Rebuild works from committed slim records plus snapshot metadata when the ignored full-body cache is absent. With that cache present it also reclassifies bodies. `pnpm run refresh` collects a new observation from GitHub; results may change as reports are filed, relabelled, closed or reopened. The original scripts and 31-check verification still apply only to frozen part 1.
