## October context

The [1.4.4 notes](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.4.4) include `bun fmt`, package-installation changes, and a SQL rework. They report **133 package-manager issue closures** and **51 SQL issue closures**, plus many merged or replaced PRs. They also list runtime stability fixes and an integer-sort regression from 1.4.0.

These are maintainer claims. Their issue counts can use different dates and rules from this study. Many closures near 22 October can reflect batch review and accumulated work. They do not by themselves show a sudden change in defect frequency or developer output. Closure dates in the issue data are needed to check that timing.

## Limits

- After **20 August**, `1.4.x` text can refer to stable, canary, a comparison build, or a dependency. It does not prove the channel.
- A snapshot stores the latest state, not every state change. `closed_at` and `state_reason` support an estimated history. Reopened and reclosed issues can change that estimate. Complete history requires [timeline events](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/issues/timeline).
- “Marked as fixed” means currently closed as completed. It does not prove a tested fix or inclusion in a release. Labels can change after a report is opened.
- Newer reports have less time to close. Different problem types can also change closure shares and delays. Equal follow-up windows reduce the first difference, not the second.
- Use and reporting frequency are unmeasured. Report growth therefore cannot establish defect growth or an upper bound on it.
- GitHub search has a [1,000-result limit per query](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/search/search#about-search). Split large ranges, reject incomplete results, and check unique counts.
- **30 October is partial**. Use the actual collection time. Do not treat the last day as complete. Before-merge states were observed in August; after-merge states were observed in October.
