## Why October needs a separate interpretation

[The 1.4.4 notes](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.4.4) describe a broad release with
`bun fmt`, package-manager changes, and a SQL rework. They report closure of 133
package-manager issues and 51 SQL issues, as well as large batches of merged or
superseded PRs. They also list runtime stability fixes and an integer-sort
regression dating to 1.4.0. These are maintainers' release claims; their issue
counts need not equal this study's cohorts or bug filter. A closure spike near
22 October can reflect batch triage and accumulated work, rather than a sudden
change in defect incidence or developer productivity. Establishing that timing
requires the issue snapshot and closure dates.

## Interpretation and collection limits

- **After 20 August, `1.4.x` no longer means canary.** An issue mentioning that
  version can refer to stable, canary, a comparison build, or a dependency.
  Lineage text matches should not be presented as verified channel assignment.
- **A snapshot is not a state-transition history.** Current `closed_at` and
  `state_reason` support a current disposition and a simplified historical
  reconstruction. Reopened and reclosed issues can invalidate the reconstructed
  earlier backlog. Complete reconstruction requires the
  [issue timeline events](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/issues/timeline).
- **Closed as completed is a proxy for fixed.** It does not prove that the fix
  was independently reproduced or included in a particular release. Current
  labels also need not be the labels present when an issue was filed.
- **Closure shares remain age- and mix-sensitive.** More recent cohorts have
  less time to close, and a changed severity mix can alter both closure share
  and median closure delay. Equal follow-up windows reduce the first problem,
  not the second. Neither rate is automatically population-independent.
- **Reports do not identify defect density.** Exposure and propensity to report
  are unmeasured. Without bounds on their changes, an observed increase in
  reports is not a mathematical upper bound on an increase in defect density.
- **GitHub search is bounded.** Its
  [documented limit](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/search/search#about-search)
  is 1,000 results per query. Fetches should split oversized intervals, reject
  `incomplete_results`, and verify unique counts before accepting a snapshot.
- **30 October is a partial observation day.** A pull made during the day must
  state its actual timestamp. A full-day average must not silently count that
  partial day as complete. The pre-merge baseline remains the original frozen
  August snapshot; its current-state closure share is therefore observed at a
  different date from the newly pulled post-merge cohort.
